 In this chapter, you will learn about the  three  areas  of  the  SCREDITOR  III
 screen  display, what each is used for, and what type of information will appear
 in each.

 More information about each section of the display will also be covered in later
 chapters of this manual.

 Refer  to  FIGURE  TWO  at  the  back  of this manual as you read and study this
 chapter.


WHAT YOU WILL SEE WITH SCREDITOR III

 As  was  discussed  in  the  introduction,  SCREDITOR  III is a window-type word
 processor. Window editing can be the  easiest  way  in  which  to  prepare  your
 documents, since you are seeing a visual image of the final document.

 In addition to looking through the window at your text, though, it is helpful to
 have additional information displayed continuously as  you  work.  In  SCREDITOR
 III,  we  have  a  Banner  Line  which tells you when you are in special editing
 modes, tells you just where your cursor is on the text in the window, and so on.
 Referring  to FIGURE TWO which folds out from the back of this manual, a special
 line called the Banner line is the top line on the screen.


THE BANNER LINE

 The  left  portion of the banner line displays general information about editing
 modes. These modes will be discussed more fully later. For now, let us just  say
 that  a  mode  is a way in which a program operates. For instance, SCREDITOR III
 will operate differently when it is in MULTI-COLUMN MODE than when  it  is  not.
 For  this  reason,  you need to know what modes are active as you enter and edit
 your text. FIGURE TWO shows all of the mode flags which SCREDITOR III  displays.
 In  actual use, some of these flags will be displayed and others will not as you
 change modes during editing. When a mode is inactive, its flag will  be  removed
 from  the  banner line, or a different mode flag will be displayed in its place.
 These flags will be discussed in detail later.

 Moving across the banner line, you will see a  series  of  letters  followed  by
 numbers.  These  numbers  tell you where your cursor is in the window and in the
 document, and where the window is in relation to the document.

 The first number is the CURSOR COLUMN. This column number is referenced from the
 left edge of your document. If, for instance, the display window is moved to the
 right so that the first column displayed is the fiftieth  column  of  the  line,
 then  when  the  cursor is at the left side of the display, the number displayed
 will be column fifty.

 The second is the CURSOR ROW. This row number is relative to the first  line  of
 the  display  window.  In other words, when the cursor is on the top line of the
 display window (the third physical line on the screen), this number will be one.

 The next number is the LINE NUMBER on which the cursor is sitting.  This  number
 is  relative  to the first line of the document you are editing, which is always
 line one. These numbers are used for such things as moving or copying text  from
 one place to another within the document.

 To  the right of the line number is the TOTAL LINES COUNT. This is the number of
 lines which exist in the document you are editing. In the  case  of  very  large
 documents  which  will  not  fit entirely within memory at one time, this number
 will be the line number of the last line in memory at a given time.  More  lines
 may exist in the document in the disk file.

 Finally,  the  right-most  numbers  are the DISPLAY WINDOW RANGE. This tells you
 where the window lies horizontally on your document. The  first  number  is  the
 left-most  column  displayed,  and  the  second  is the right-most column in the
 display window.

 All of the information in the banner line will  change  constantly  as  you  use
 SCREDITOR III, as you will see as you progress through this manual.
THE TAB/MARGIN/COMMAND/ERROR LINE

 Whenever the second line on the screen is not being  used  for  something  else,
 SCREDITOR  III  uses that line to display any margins or tab settings within the
 currently-displayed window. As the window  move  back  and  forth  across  lines
 longer  than  the  width  of the screen, this line, which we call the TAB/MARGIN
 LINE, will automatically change to reflect the settings for the part of the line
 being shown in the window. Any time a margin or tab is changed, the line is also
 updated. Any column which does not have a tab or margin setting will shown as  a
 period, which is used as a place-holder.

 The  tabs are displayed as upper-case T's in each tabbed column. The margins are
 shown as an upper-case L for the left margin, and an upper-case R for the  right
 margin.  If  both  margins  are  in  the  same  column,  an upper-case B will be
 displayed. These margins are important, since many of SCREDITOR III's operations
 will only take place within the margins, as will be discussed later.

 If  an  error occurs which requires that you know exactly what went wrong during
 the operation of SCREDITOR III, the tab/margin line  will  be  cleared  and  the
 error message will be displayed in its place.

 Many  of SCREDITOR III's operations require that you enter more information than
 a single  keystroke  would  allow.  When  you  wish  to  execute  any  of  these
 operations,  called  COMMANDS  (which  will  be  discussed  in detail later) the
 TAB/MARGIN line will be cleared so that the desired COMMAND may  be  entered  on
 this line of the screen. While the line is being used this way, it is called the
 COMMAND LINE.


THE SCREEN DISPLAY WINDOW

 The  remaining lines of the screen are used for the screen display window. It is
 in this area of the screen that you will actually see your  text.  As  has  been
 previously  mentioned,  the text you see is a visual representation of what your
 printed document will look like. The actual size of the  SCREEN  DISPLAY  WINDOW
 will vary depending upon the number of rows and columns which your terminal will
 display.


SUMMARY

 In this chapter, you have learned:

 1) THE BANNER LINE CONSTANTLY DISPLAYS IMPORTANT INFORMATION ABOUT WHERE YOU ARE
    AND WHAT SCREDITOR III IS DOING.


 2) THE TAB/MARGIN/COMMAND/ERROR LINE IS USED FOR:

    A. DISPLAYING TABS AND MARGINS.
    B. DISPLAYING ERROR MESSAGES.
    C. ENTERING AND DISPLAYING COMMANDS.


 3) THE  REST OF THE SCREEN IS JUST LIKE A WINDOW THROUGH WHICH YOUR DOCUMENT CAN
    BE SEEN.
