 This chapter will focus on how you position the cursor in  the  current  display
 window. You will learn all of SCREDITOR III's cursor movement OPERATORS.

 In  order  to  complete  this  chapter,  you will need to know where each of the
 cursoring SINGLE-KEY OPERATORS are located on your keyboard. Since  this  varies
 from keyboard to keyboard, each OPERATOR will be referred to by name rather than
 the actual key you will press to enter it. When SCREDITOR III was  installed  in
 your  system,  a  list  should  have  been  made  of the location of each of the
 SINGLE-KEY OPERATORS on your keyboard. You will need to study this list  as  you
 read  this  chapter,  and  should  attempt  to  memorize  their  positions.  The
 SINGLE-KEY OPERATORS which you will be using are:


    ^CR - CURSOR RIGHT
    ^CL - CURSOR LEFT
    ^CD - CURSOR DOWN
    ^CU - CURSOR UP
    ^TR - TAB RIGHT
    ^TL - TAB LEFT
    ^TN - TAB NEXT WORD
    ^TP - TAB  TO  PREVIOUS  WORD
    ^TM - TAB TO MARGIN
    ^TE - TAB TO END OF LINE


BEFORE YOU CAN START

 The  cursor  is  the  most  important visual feature on your screen when you use
 SCREDITOR III. The cursor is the pointer to the location on the screen at  which
 text  will  be  entered  as  you  type,  and  often tells you where a COMMAND or
 OPERATOR will start or stop.

 The location on your keyboard of the cursor movement OPERATORS should be quickly
 memorized  since  cursor movement will be used more often than any other feature
 of SCREDITOR III!


      * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * *
      *                                                                   *
      *   CURSOR  MOVEMENT OPERATORS ARE THE MOST-OFTEN USED FEATURE OF   *
      *   WINDOW EDITING PROGRAMS BECAUSE PRACTICALLY EVERYTHING YOU DO   *
      *   WITH SUCH A PROGRAM TAKES PLACE AT, ENDS AT, OR STARTS AT THE   *
      *   CURSOR.                                                         *
      *                                                                   *
      * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * *


 In  this  chapter you will be using the NEWDOC file that you created previously;
 so, to start with, open a new edit session on the NEWDOC file.


CURSOR MOVEMENT OPERATORS

 Locate the CURSOR UP, CURSOR DOWN, CURSOR LEFT and  CURSOR  RIGHT  OPERATORS  on
 your keyboard.

 Try  entering  the CURSOR DOWN OPERATOR. Note that the cursor moves down one row
 each time you type it, and that the banner reflects the changed row number  each
 time.  Continue  typing  the  OPERATOR until you reach the bottom of the screen.
 Once you are on the last line of the screen, your terminal bell will  ring  each
 time you enter this OPERATOR.


      * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * *
      *                                                                   *
      *        NOTE THAT CURSOR MOVEMENT DOES NOT CHANGE ANY TEXT.        *
      *                                                                   *
      * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * *


 Now use the CURSOR UP OPERATOR in the same manner. Once you reach the top of the
 display window, the bell will ring when you enter the OPERATOR.

 Now use the CURSOR RIGHT OPERATOR. When you try to pass the  right  margin,  the
 bell will again ring.

 Use  the  CURSOR  LEFT  OPERATOR, and notice that the same thing occurs once you
 reach the left margin.


      * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * *
      *                                                                   *
      *   CURSOR MOVEMENT IS ALWAYS WITHIN THE CURRENT MARGINS AND  THE   *
      *   TOP  AND BOTTOM OF THE CURRENT SCREEN WINDOW. YOU CANNOT MOVE   *
      *   THE CURSOR OUTSIDE THESE LIMITS.                                *
      *                                                                   *
      * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * *


 If you are not there already, use the cursor movement  OPERATORS  to  place  the
 cursor on ROW 001, COLUMN 001.


CURSOR TABBING OPERATORS

 Locate the TAB RIGHT, TAB LEFT, and TAB TO MARGIN OPERATORS on your keyboard.

 Now enter the TAB RIGHT OPERATOR. Note that the cursor moves to the first tab to
 the  right  of its old position, or COLUMN 011 in this case. Continue typing the
 TAB RIGHT OPERATOR. Verify that when there are no tabs between the  current  tab
 setting  and  the  right margin, the cursor will move to the right margin and if
 you enter the TAB RIGHT OPERATOR at the right margin, the bell will ring.

 Enter  the  TAB LEFT OPERATOR in the same way. As you can see, the action is the
 same as TAB RIGHT, only in reverse.

 Try typing the TAB TO MARGIN OPERATOR from a number of columns on the line. Note
 that  when  the  cursor  is  not at the left margin, this OPERATOR will move the
 cursor to the left margin. If the cursor is at the left  margin,  this  OPERATOR
 will move the cursor to the right margin.

 Now  use  the  cursor movement OPERATORS to place the cursor at the the start of
 the second line after the salutation in your letter. This  should  be  ROW  011,
 COLUMN  001. When you are there, locate and enter the TAB TO NEXT WORD OPERATOR.
 Each time you enter this OPERATOR, the cursor will move to the start of the next
 word  on the line. Verify that when the cursor is at or to the right of the last
 word on the line, the bell will ring.

 Now locate the TAB TO PREVIOUS WORD OPERATOR. Each time you type this  OPERATOR,
 note  that  the cursor moves to the start of the previous word on the line. When
 the start of the first word on the line is reached, the bell will  ring  if  the
 OPERATOR is entered again.

 Finally,  locate  and enter the TAB TO END OF LINE OPERATOR. As you can see, the
 cursor moves to the first space after the last word on the line. Try  this  from
 various  cursor positions both to the left and the right of the last word on any
 line.

 Practice using all of the cursor movement OPERATORS for a few minutes to  get  a
 feel  for their location and operation. Once you are reasonably comfortable with
 these OPERATORS, enter the ESCAPE OPERATOR, type AB, then  the  DELETE-TO-MARGIN
 OPERATOR, and finally the NEW LINE OPERATOR to abort the edit session.


SUMMARY

 The following are special points to remember about this chapter:

 1) CURSOR  MOVEMENT  IS ALWAYS WITHIN THE CURRENT MARGINS AND THE TOP AND BOTTOM
    OF THE CURRENT SCREEN WINDOW.  YOU  CANNOT  MOVE  THE  CURSOR  OUTSIDE  THESE
    LIMITS.

 2) CURSOR MOVEMENT DOES NOT CHANGE ANY TEXT.

 3) CURSOR MOVEMENT OPERATORS ARE USED MOST OFTEN WITH  WINDOW  EDITING  PROGRAMS
    BECAUSE PRACTICALLY EVERYTHING YOU DO WITH A WINDOW-TYPE WORD PROCESSOR TAKES
    PLACE AT, ENDS AT, OR STARTS AT THE CURSOR.
